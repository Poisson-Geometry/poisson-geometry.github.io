About Poisson Geometry

An international meeting place for a field shaped by connections.

The mathematics

A Poisson structure equips the smooth functions on a manifold with a bilinear, antisymmetric bracket. The bracket satisfies the Jacobi identity and a product rule called the Leibniz rule.

{f, gh} = {f, g}h + g{f, h}

Symplectic geometry gives important examples, but Poisson structures also describe spaces where the rank of the geometry varies. Their symplectic leaves, symmetries and deformations lead to questions across geometry and mathematical physics.

Topics in Poisson geometry

The following is a selection of active and interconnected research directions, from the local geometry of Poisson manifolds to their roles in dynamics, quantization, and higher geometry.

Geometry, foliations and Lie theory

Symplectic leaves and foliations. Every Poisson manifold is partitioned into immersed symplectic leaves, whose dimensions may jump from point to point. The resulting (possibly singular) foliation links Poisson geometry to foliation theory, holonomy, and the global topology of leaf spaces.

Local normal forms and Poisson linearization. Weinstein's splitting theorem separates the symplectic and transverse directions near a point. Linearization asks when the transverse Poisson structure is equivalent to its first-order Lie–Poisson model; rigidity, isotropy, and obstructions are central to this question.

Lie algebroids, Lie groupoids, and integration. A Poisson structure gives the cotangent bundle a natural Lie algebroid structure. When this algebroid is integrable, its global counterpart is a symplectic groupoid, connecting infinitesimal Poisson data with global geometry; not every Poisson manifold admits such an integration.

Dirac structures and Courant algebroids. Dirac geometry unifies Poisson bivectors and closed two-forms as involutive, maximally isotropic subbundles of the generalized tangent bundle. Courant algebroids provide the ambient framework for these structures and for reduction, duality, and generalized geometry.

Generalized complex geometry. Generalized complex structures combine features of complex and symplectic geometry within the Courant-algebroid framework. They have an underlying real Poisson structure and connect to holomorphic geometry, deformation theory, and aspects of mirror symmetry.

Poisson–Lie groups and Lie bialgebras. Poisson–Lie groups are Lie groups whose multiplication is compatible with a Poisson structure, while Lie bialgebras encode their infinitesimal data. Classical r-matrices and the Yang–Baxter equation connect this theory to integrable systems, representation theory, and quantum groups.

Algebraic, singular and higher structures

Poisson cohomology and deformation theory. The Poisson bivector defines a cohomology theory that records infinitesimal symmetries and deformations of the bracket. Questions of rigidity, stability, and obstruction theory connect Poisson geometry with homological algebra and higher Lie structures.

Deformation quantization. Deformation quantization replaces the commutative product of functions with an associative star product whose first-order commutator recovers the Poisson bracket. It provides a precise bridge from classical observables to noncommutative and quantum geometry.

Cluster Poisson varieties. Many cluster varieties carry Poisson brackets that take a simple log-canonical form in cluster coordinate charts and transform compatibly under mutation. This links Poisson geometry to higher Teichmüller theory, character varieties, representation theory, and quantum cluster algebras.

Shifted symplectic and Poisson geometry. Derived geometry allows symplectic and Poisson structures to live in nonzero cohomological degrees on derived spaces and stacks. Shifted structures naturally appear on moduli spaces, quotient stacks, and derived intersections, with higher Lie groupoids providing geometric models in the differentiable setting.

Holomorphic and singular Poisson structures. Holomorphic Poisson geometry studies brackets compatible with complex-analytic or algebraic structures. Log-symplectic and other singular Poisson structures allow controlled degeneracies, revealing links to divisors, characteristic classes, and the topology of singular loci.

Jacobi and contact geometry. Jacobi structures generalize Poisson brackets by relaxing the ordinary Leibniz rule and naturally encompass contact geometry. Their relation to Poisson structures through Poissonization makes them part of a broader family of geometric brackets.

Dynamics, reduction and mathematical physics

Hamiltonian dynamics and integrable systems. A Hamiltonian function on a Poisson manifold determines a vector field tangent to its symplectic leaves. The Poisson bracket organizes conserved quantities and commuting flows, making Poisson geometry a natural setting for completely integrable systems and geometric mechanics.

Symmetries, moment maps and reduction. Hamiltonian symmetries and moment maps allow one to construct reduced Poisson or symplectic spaces from systems with symmetry. Group-valued moment maps, quasi-Poisson structures, and singular reduction extend these constructions beyond the classical symplectic framework.

Geometric numerical integration. Poisson integrators are numerical methods designed to preserve the Poisson bracket, rather than merely approximate trajectories. Such methods seek to respect the qualitative geometry of Hamiltonian dynamics, including symplectic leaves and Casimir functions.

Moduli spaces and character varieties. Moduli spaces of flat connections, surface-group representations, and related geometric structures often carry natural Poisson or symplectic brackets. Their study brings together gauge theory, representation theory, reduction, and quantization.

Poisson sigma models and field theory. The Poisson sigma model connects Poisson manifolds with two-dimensional topological field theory and the geometry of symplectic groupoids. BV–BFV and AKSZ methods extend this connection to quantization, boundaries, and higher or shifted geometric structures.

These themes are reflected in the scientific programmes of recent international Poisson meetings, including Poisson 2024 and Poisson 2026.

The international community

The Poisson Geometry organization maintains this website as a shared resource for the international community. It brings together the history of the international Poisson conference series and information about forthcoming meetings.

The series began in Warsaw in 1998. Meetings are hosted in different parts of the world and bring together researchers at all career stages. Recent editions combine a school with a research conference.

Each edition has its own organizing committee. This website records those historical committees without implying that past organizers hold current roles in the organization.
