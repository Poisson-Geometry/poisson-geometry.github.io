Post-Lie algebras
      Definition (Vallette)
      A post-Lie algebra (g, [·, ·]g , ) consists of a Lie algebra (g, [·, ·]g )
      and a binary product  : g ⊗ g → g such that

                           x  [y, z]g = [x  y, z]g + [y, x  z]g ,
                           [x, y]g  z = a (x, y, z) − a (y, x, z),

      where a (x, y, z) = x  (y  z) − (x  y)  z.

             B. Vallette, Homology of generalized partition posets, J. Pure Appl.
             Algebra 208 (2007), 699-725.

Post-Lie algebras
      Proposition

      A post-Lie algebra (g, [·, ·]g , ) gives rise to a new Lie algebra
      (g, [·, ·] ), called the sub-adjacent Lie algebra and denoted by
      g , where the Lie bracket [·, ·] is defined by

                         [x, y] = [x, y]g + x  y − y  x, ∀x, y ∈ g.

      Moreover, L : (g, [·, ·] ) → Der(g, [·, ·]g ) is an action of the
      sub-adjacent Lie algebra g on the original Lie algebra (g, [·, ·]g ).

            In a post-Lie algebra (g, [·, ·]g , ), if the Lie bracket [·, ·]g is
      trivial, then we obtain a pre-Lie algebra, namely a vector space g
      with a multiplication  satisfying
                                     a (x, y, z) − a (y, x, z) = 0.

Post-Lie algebras


      Example

      Let (g, [·, ·]g ) be a Lie algebra, (A, ·) a commutative associative
      algebra and ρ : g → Der(A) a Lie algebra homomorphism.
      Then there is a post-Lie algebra structure on A ⊗ g, which is
      given by

                                    [a ⊗ x, b ⊗ y] = ab ⊗ [x, y]g ,
                             (a ⊗ x)  (b ⊗ y) = a · ρ(x)(b) ⊗ y,

      for all a, b ∈ A, x, y ∈ g.

             There is a meta theorem in the regularity structures.

      The Hopf algebra in Martin Hairer’s regularity structures encoding
      the combinatorics of recentering is obtained as the universal
      envelope of a suitable post-Lie algebra.

Applications in braces


          A brace (G, ◦, ·) consists of an abelian group (G, ·) and a
      group (G, ◦) such that

                  a ◦ (b · c) = (a ◦ b) · a·−1 · (a ◦ c),                  ∀a, b, c ∈ G.

           Smoktunowicz proposed the following questions:
           Question. Is there a passage from all left nilpotent braces of
      cardinality pn , with n + 1 < p, to left nilpotent pre-Lie rings?
             A. Smoktunowicz, On the passage from finite braces to pre-Lie rings.
             Adv. Math. 409 (2022), 108683.

      A post-group is a group (G, ·) equipped with another binary

         1    for all a ∈ G, the left multiplication

      Definition (Guo-Lang-S.)

      Rota-Baxter Lie algebra of weight 1.

Skew-left braces

      Definition (Rump)
      A skew-left brace (G, ◦, ·) consists of a group (G, ·) and a group
      (G, ◦) such that

                  a ◦ (b · c) = (a ◦ b) · a·−1 · (a ◦ c),                 ∀a, b, c ∈ G.

             W. Rump, A decomposition theorem for square-free unitary solutions of
             the quantum Yang-Baxter equation. Adv. Math. 193 (2005), 40-55.

             T. Gateva-Ivanova, Set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation,
             braces and symmetric groups. Adv. Math. 338 (2018), 649-701.

             F. Cedó, A. Smoktunowicz and L. Vendramin, Skew left braces of
             nilpotent type. Proc. Lond. Math. Soc. 118 (2019), 1367-1392.

      Definition
      A post-Lie algebroid structure on a vector bundle A −→ M is a
      triple that consists of a C ∞ (M )-linear Lie algebra structure [·, ·]A
      on Γ(A), a bilinear operation .A : Γ(A) ⊗ Γ(A) −→ Γ(A) and a
      vector bundle morphism aA : A −→ T M , called the anchor, such
      that (Γ(A), [·, ·]A , .A ) is a post-Lie algebra, and for all
      f ∈ C ∞ (M ) and u, v ∈ Γ(A), the following relations are satisfied:
        (i) u .A (f v) = f (u .A v) + aA (u)(f )v,
      (ii) (f u) .A v = f (u .A v).

             H. Z. Munthe-Kaas and A. Lundervold, On post-Lie algebras, Lie-Butcher
             series and moving frames, Found. Comput. Math. 13 (2013), 583-613.
